FAQ

ChartJam · last updated 22 November 2026

Getting started

›What is ChartJam?

A practice app for musicians: it shows lead sheets (melody, chords and form) and plays a band behind them, piano, bass, guitar and drums in the style you choose, in any key and at any tempo. It runs in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer.

›Do I need an account?

No. Open the app and start. Your songs and settings stay on your device.

›Can I install it like an app?

Yes. In Chrome or Edge choose "Install app" in the address bar or menu; on an iPhone or iPad, tap Share and "Add to Home Screen". It then opens in its own window and works offline.

›Can I use ChartJam in my language?

ChartJam follows your device's language when it has it (English, Dutch, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese and Italian). The globe button in the library's row of buttons chooses another one. The legal pages are in English only.

›Does it work offline?

Yes, once a page and its sounds have loaded while online. Each instrument's sounds download the first time they play and are kept, so play a song with your usual style and melody instrument once before you go offline.

Songs and files

›Where do songs come from?

From ChartJam files (.chartjam): a song or a collection of songs someone shared with you, or that you exported on another device. Import them with the file button on the home page. You can also write a new song with the + button, in the score editor.

Or type a chord chart, only the chords and the form: the + button, then Chord chart. Chord charts are an experimental feature for now: switch them on under Experimental features, at the foot of the home page.

›Can I write a chord chart without the melody?

Yes: tap + on the home page and choose Chord chart. Type the chords bar by bar: | between bars, the chords of a bar separated by spaces (they share it evenly), . to give the chord before one more share (Bb7 . . A7 is three beats and one), and % for a bar that repeats the one before. |: and :| are repeats, |1 and :|2 the endings, [A] labels a section, and Segno, Coda, To Coda, Fine, D.S. al Coda and D.C. al Fine go in their bar as words. The chart is drawn as you type, in slash notation, with any mistake listed and its bar in red; Play lets you hear it before you save.

A chord chart is a song like any other: it plays with the band in any key and style, reads for your instrument, and goes in setlists, stage mode and the booklet. The pencil in the player opens its chords as text again. On a phone, a row of keys above the text box types the signs, and Chord opens the chord pad. Chord charts are free. Chord charts are an experimental feature for now: switch them on under Experimental features, at the foot of the home page.

›Can I share songs, a collection or a setlist?

Yes, with the Share button (three joined dots): on a song's row in the library or in the player, on a setlist (on the Setlists page or in the setlist) and in a collection's ⋯ menu (choose it under Filters). It opens your device's own share sheet (AirDrop, Messages, WhatsApp, mail…). A song goes as a link that holds the whole song: whoever taps it, even without ChartJam, sees it in the player at once, in any key and with the band, and can keep it. The song travels in the link itself and never passes through a server, ours included. A setlist or collection goes as a ChartJam file with its songs (and a setlist's keys, tempos and notes). On a Windows computer the link is copied instead, ready to paste, and a setlist or collection is downloaded; where there is no share sheet the file is downloaded. Export (the download buttons) still makes the file. The songs that come with ChartJam can't be shared.

Anyone with ChartJam imports it with the file button on the home page, or a setlist with Import on the Setlists page. New songs are added. If a song, setlist or collection is already in their library and differs from the file, ChartJam asks: keep theirs, use the file's, or keep both (the file's as a copy). Nothing is overwritten without asking.

Receiving: a song link opens the song wherever it's tapped. In the installed app on Android, ChartJam appears in the share sheet, so a ChartJam file shared to it from WhatsApp, mail or Files opens straight in ChartJam; on a computer, the installed app opens .chartjam files (double-click, or Open with). Anything else goes through the file button on the home page.

›Can I open a ChartJam file in another app?

No. ChartJam files are ChartJam's own format: your songs and backups open in ChartJam, on any of your devices, and nowhere else.

›Why isn't my favourite standard included?

Most standards are protected by copyright: their melodies and lyrics can't be shared without the publishers' permission. We only include songs in the public domain and our own practice material. Charts you write or import yourself are yours to use.

The songs that come with ChartJam are marked Included in the library. They can't be deleted, only archived, and editing one saves your own copy; their settings, tags, notes and collections are yours to change. If you already had one of them from a shared file, the included song takes its place, with your settings and in your setlists and collections. Their charts can't be exported, and neither can your copies of them: a backup, or a collection or setlist you share, holds only your settings, tags and notes for them, which go back on the same songs in the other ChartJam. Your copies stay on this device. PDF and PNG exports of the sheet work as for any song.

›How do I keep my songs safe, or move them to another device?

Your library lives only in this browser on this device. Use the download button on the home page to save a backup (a ChartJam file) now and then, and the upload button to restore it, on the same device or another one. Backups made before the ChartJam format (.json) still restore. Restore with Merge asks about songs that differ from yours, as an import does; Replace makes the library exactly the backup. Clearing the browser's data for the site deletes the library, so keep a backup.

›Can I group songs, or make a setlist for a gig?

Yes. Collections group songs (e.g. per band); choose one under Filters, next to the library's search. Setlists, behind the list icon on the home page, put songs in gig order with a key, tempo and note per song; playing a setlist steps through it with the previous and next buttons.

Songs you don't play any more can be archived with the box button on their row: they leave the library's list (and its previous/next) without being deleted, and stay in the setlists and collections that hold them. Under Filters, next to the search, the Active / Archived / All switch shows the archived songs, or all of them, to bring one back. The switch next to it shows only the included songs or only your own, and the style tags only the songs playing the styles you tap (one or several).

Playing

›How do I choose the style, tempo and count-in?

In the Playback card of the player's side panel (on a phone, the controls button): Backing Style, the tempo, the count-in and how many times the song repeats. Each song remembers its own settings. When the song ends (not when it repeats endlessly), the band rounds it off: a fill, then a final chord that rings out.

Styles you don't use can be archived on the Styles page (the styles icon on the home page): they leave the Backing Style menu without being deleted, and a song that plays one keeps it.

›Can I hear the melody?

Yes: switch Melody on in the Playback card and pick its sound (piano, a horn, woodwind, vibes, Rhodes, organ…). The Mixer sets its level and that of each part of the band. When the song repeats, switch on Melody 1st time in the Playback card: the melody plays the first time through, as the head, and then the band plays on for your solos.

›How do I start from a certain bar, or loop a few bars?

Stopped or paused, tap a bar and Play starts there; while playing, double-tap a bar to jump to it. To loop, press the loop button next to Play, then tap the first and the last bar of the loop.

›The highlight runs ahead of or behind what I hear.

With Bluetooth headphones or speakers the sound arrives later. Set Bluetooth Delay in the Playback card until the highlight matches the sound.

›Can I use ChartJam on stage, with a foot pedal?

Yes: the stage button next to Play (four corners) shows the chart full screen, under a slim bar, and keeps the screen on. The sheet turns half a page at a time: the line in the middle of the screen moves to the top, so you read on while the next lines come in. Turn with → ↓ or Page Down (back with ← ↑ or Page Up), with a Bluetooth page-turn pedal, which sends those keys, or by tapping the sheet: its left third turns back, the rest forward.

At the end of the sheet a turn shows the next song (of the setlist you're playing, else of the library), and one more opens it, still full screen; the ‹ › buttons in the bar do the same. The moon button makes the sheet dark, for a dark stage (the PDF and PNG stay white). Esc or the button at the top left leaves stage mode.

›What are the experimental features?

Features we're still working on, which you can try before they're finished. The Experimental features link at the foot of the home page lists them, each with a switch; they're all off until you switch one on, on that device, and they may still change or go away.

The first is Feel controls: a Feel box in the Playback card that sets how far the band swings (straight to hard), whether it plays ahead of the beat, on it or laid back, how much the piano and guitar comp (sparse, normal or busy), and whether the drums play simple (no ghost notes, fewer fills). It works with every style, is saved per song, and is part of Pro. Switched off, the band plays as usual and each song's feel is kept for later.

Harmonic analysis writes under every chord what it does in the key, as Roman numerals (ii7, V7, Imaj7; V7/ii for a secondary dominant, subV7 for a tritone substitute) or Nashville numbers (2m7, 5⁷): switch it on with Analysis in the Training card. Each ii–V gets a bracket under it, and where a ii–V–I moves to another key for a moment, that key is named (→ F) and those chords are numbered in it. The numbers are the same in every key and for every instrument, and print in the PDF. Pro. Chord charts are an experiment too (see "Can I write a chord chart without the melody?").

›Can the band play softer and louder?

Yes, when the chart says so. In the score editor, select a note and use the Dynamics row of the Marks pane: pp, p, mp, mf, f or ff from that note on, < or > to start a crescendo or diminuendo, and ⊣ on a later note to end it. In a chord chart, write p, f and the like, cresc. or dim. as words in a bar.

The band and the melody follow the marks as they play: a level holds until the next one, and a crescendo or diminuendo grows or fades note by note to the level written after it (or one level on). After a repeat or a D.S. the band is as loud as the marks it has passed say. Without marks it plays as always. The marks show on the chart and in the PDF.

›Why does ChartJam ask for my microphone?

Only for the tuner: the tuning fork in the player's header opens it. It asks the first time you start it, and listens only while the tuner is open and listening: the sound is measured on your device to show the note you play, flat, in tune or sharp, and is never recorded, kept or sent. Its Tone tab plays a reference tone to tune to by ear instead. Both read in your instrument's written pitch, with A = 440 or another reference pitch. Free.

Keys and instruments

›How do I change the key?

Pick another key in the Key menu. The chart and the band both move to the new key.

›I play trumpet, sax or horn. Can I see my part?

Yes: choose your instrument in the Instrument menu. The chart is written for it (B♭, E♭ or F, or bass clef for trombone), fitted into its range, and the Key menu shows the key as you read it, with the concert key in brackets. The chart says which part it is at the top left, as a printed part does, also in the PDF and PNG. The band always plays in concert pitch, so you can play along as you read.

Practice tools

›What is ear training?

In the Training card: the notes of the melody are hidden while it plays, so you learn the tune by ear and still see the chords and form.

The Training card can also switch off the Cursor, the mark on the bar that sounds, so you keep your place yourself.

›Can I see the chord tones, or which scale to play over each chord?

Yes: switch on Chord tones in the Training card (a song with one voice). A staff under the melody shows every chord's notes, from the root up, spread over the chord's length. Choose Scale under the switch to see instead the scale that fits each chord, from its root to the octave, with the guide tones, the 3rd and 7th that make the changes heard, in red.

The scale is picked from the chord and its place in the key: Dorian on a ii, Phrygian on a iii, Mixolydian on a dominant, Lydian on a IV, the altered scale on a 7alt, half-whole diminished on a 7♭9, Phrygian dominant on a dominant going to a minor chord, and so on. It follows the key and your instrument and is in the PDF; the band doesn't play it.

›What does the speed trainer do?

In the Training card: the song loops and gets a little faster each time round, from a start tempo up to an end tempo, so you can work a tune up to speed.

›Can I hide the lyrics?

Yes: for a song with lyrics, the Lyrics switch in the Training card hides them. The song itself isn't changed.

›Can I make my own backing style?

Yes, behind the styles icon on the home page: start a new style or copy a built-in one, and set the drums, bass, piano and guitar hits bar by bar. Your own styles appear in the Backing Style menu.

›Can I record myself playing along?

Yes, with Record yourself, an experimental Pro feature (switch it on under Experimental features). A red Record button appears next to Play: the song plays from the top with its count-in and the microphone records you until you stop or the song ends. Use headphones, so the take holds only you. The Takes card in the controls keeps each song's last 5 takes (a starred one stays) and plays them back with the band, exactly as it played then, or on their own, with a level for each. Test the timing once per device (speakers on, no headphones) so your takes line up with the band. Takes stay on your device, aren't in backups or shared songs, and leave it only when you share or download one, as an audio file of your part.

Editing and exporting

›How do I fix a wrong note or chord?

Open the editor with the pencil button in the player. Tap a note to select it and change it with the tools or the keyboard (the ? button lists the shortcuts). The toolbar opens the tools in panes (notes, rhythm, marks, bars, form, song) that you can drag anywhere on the screen.

›Can I make the notes bigger?

Yes: zoom with the − and + buttons in the corner of the sheet, in the player and in the editor (each remembers its own zoom), or with Ctrl and the mouse wheel. Tap the percentage to fit the chart to the width again.

›Can I copy a song to make my own version?

Yes: the copy button on a song in the library, or next to Edit in the player, makes "<title> (copy)" with the same chart and settings, and opens it in the editor to change. The original stays as it was, and so do the setlists and collections it's in. A copy of an included song stays on your device, like the song itself.

›Can I write on a chart?

Yes: the pen button in the bottom left corner of the sheet opens the annotation tools: a pen, a marker that highlights under the notes, text, and an eraser, with colours, undo and redo. Your marks are kept with the song and stay on their bars however the chart is laid out: zoomed, with the chord tones shown, in another key or for another instrument, and when you add or delete bars in the editor.

On a tablet a stylus always draws; once you've used one, your fingers scroll the sheet again. The eye button next to the pen hides the marks, also in the PDF and PNG exports. They go with the song in backups and shared files.

›Is there a light mode?

Yes. The app follows your device's light or dark setting; the half-filled circle in the library's row of buttons switches between that, light and dark. The chart itself is always black on white.

›Can I print a chart?

Yes: export it as a PDF or a PNG image with the buttons at the bottom of the player's panel.

›Can I print a whole setlist?

Yes: a setlist's PDF button (on the Setlists page, or in the setlist itself) makes a booklet, one PDF with a front page and every chart. The front page has the setlist's name and date and the contents: each song's title, key, tempo, note and page. Each song starts on a new page, in the setlist's key for it and written for your instrument, with your annotations unless you've hidden them.

Price

›What does it cost?

ChartJam is free to use. Pro, with every backing style, the mixer and the melody, the score editor, setlists and more, is coming soon: see what it adds.

Privacy and help

›What do you know about me?

Next to nothing: no account, no tracking cookies, no profile of you. A cookie-free counter (Cloudflare Web Analytics) only counts page visits. Your library stays on your device. See the privacy policy.

›I don't hear anything.

Turn the volume up and check the right output is chosen; on an iPhone, the silent switch can mute the app, so switch it off. Browsers only start sound after a tap, so press Play yourself. The first time, the sounds still have to download, which takes a moment on a slow connection.
